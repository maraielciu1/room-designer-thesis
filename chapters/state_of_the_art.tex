\chapter{Related Work in Mobile Room Capture and 3D Scene Representation}
\label{ch:soa}

This chapter surveys work that motivates and positions the system developed
in this thesis: commercial AR interior tools (Section~\ref{sec:soa:commercial}),
indoor semantic capture and RoomPlan (Section~\ref{sec:soa:indoor}), the
SLAM lineage behind depth-based room capture (Section~\ref{sec:soa:slam}),
mobile AR usability findings (Section~\ref{sec:soa:usability}), and novel-view
synthesis from NeRF to 3D Gaussian Splatting (Section~\ref{sec:soa:nvs}).
Section~\ref{sec:soa:gap} states the research gap and how this thesis closes it.

The sections below build toward Section~\ref{sec:soa:gap}: what existing systems
can and cannot do, why a non-AR editor is justified, and where Gaussian
Splatting fits the viewing pipeline.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Commercial AR Interior Design Applications}
\label{sec:soa:commercial}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Consumer AR tools such as IKEA~Place and similar viewers in Wayfair, Houzz,
and Amazon use ARKit plane detection to anchor scaled product models on the
floor, letting users walk around a composited item in the live camera feed.
They reduce purchase uncertainty for some users but do not model the room:
no walls, openings, or existing furniture are captured, and no artefact
persists beyond the session. Each preview starts from scratch and requires
the user to be on-site. Procedural layout systems such as
K{\'a}n~et~al.~\cite{kan2021ar} add automated arrangement suggestions on the
live feed but still depend on transient plane detection rather than a captured
metric room and offer no off-site persistence.

The gap these tools leave open, and that this thesis addresses, is the
absence of a single application that captures a structured, metric, persistent
room model; supports editing off-site; and authors or imports three-dimensional
assets on device.

\section{Indoor Scene Understanding and Semantic Datasets}
\label{sec:soa:indoor}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Indoor datasets such as ScanNet~\cite{dai2017scannet} and
Matterport3D~\cite{CAS+17} established that semantic 3D structure, surfaces
typed as walls, floors, and openings, plus furniture instances, is achievable
at scale from real scans and improves downstream layout and design tasks
compared with raw geometry alone. ScanNet pairs commodity RGB-D capture with
offline reconstruction and manual labelling; Matterport3D extends similar
semantics to multi-room building scale. Apple's RoomPlan API~\cite{apple2024roomplan2}
brings a consumer subset of that capability to supported devices: a
'CapturedRoom' with typed surfaces and object instances, produced
on-device in typically 1-3 minutes during an interactive scan for
average-sized rooms (though larger or more complex spaces may require longer
coverage), rather than through hours-long offline pipelines. This thesis treats RoomPlan as the
source of the metric, typed room model that anchors editing and export.

\section{Dense SLAM and Volumetric Reconstruction}
\label{sec:soa:slam}

SLAM (Simultaneous Localisation and Mapping) systems build a 3D map of an
environment while tracking where the camera is within it.
KinectFusion~\cite{newcombe2011kinectfusion} was one of the first to do
this in real time by continuously fusing depth readings into a 3D grid of
distance values, though it was limited to small, desk-sized volumes.
Later systems such as ElasticFusion~\cite{WLS+15} and
BundleFusion~\cite{DAN+17} scaled this to full rooms and were used to
capture large indoor datasets such as ScanNet~\cite{dai2017scannet}.

All of these required a desktop computer tethered to the depth camera.
ARKit moved equivalent tracking onto a phone by combining camera and
inertial sensor data, a technique called visual-inertial odometry (VIO).
Research systems such as ORB-SLAM3~\cite{CEG+21} demonstrate the maturity
of feature-based VIO; ARKit provides a proprietary implementation of the
same principle. It works well in textured scenes but degrades under fast
motion or on plain surfaces.

RoomPlan builds on ARKit's tracking but goes further: instead of raw
geometry, it outputs a structured description of the room with named
walls, doors, windows, and labelled furniture instances. This is the
representation this thesis uses for furniture replacement.

\section{Mobile AR Usability Evaluation}
\label{sec:soa:usability}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Design choices in this thesis, especially the persistence-first non-AR editor
alongside optional AR preview, follow empirical work on mobile AR interior
apps. Kandil~et~al. ~\cite{kandil2021ux} evaluated several commercial tools with
standardised questionnaires covering general usability and AR-specific factors
(fatigue from sustained holding, placement accuracy, and cognitive load from
overlaying virtual content on the real environment). The weakest dimensions
were \emph{manipulation precision} and \emph{environmental awareness}: users
struggled to position and orient virtual objects accurately while holding the
device, and transient plane anchoring weakened spatial understanding of the
real room. Participants also wanted saved staging sessions, absent from all
evaluated apps, which motivates persistent, off-site editing.
Editing in a stable 3D view grounded to a captured metric model rather than a
live AR feed targets both failure modes. This thesis does not conduct a new
usability study but maps these findings to non-functional requirements in
Chapter~\ref{ch:requirements}, and the same instruments could evaluate the
delivered system in future work.

\section{Novel View Synthesis: From NeRF to 3D Gaussian Splatting}
\label{sec:soa:nvs}

Novel view synthesis is the task of generating a photo-realistic image of
a scene from a new camera angle, given only a set of existing photographs.

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf} were an early
breakthrough: a neural network is trained on a set of photos and learns
to predict what the scene looks like from any direction. The results are
visually impressive, but training takes hours and rendering a single frame
is too slow for interactive use. 

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} takes a different
approach. Instead of a neural network, the scene is represented as a
cloud of small 3D blobs (Gaussians), each with a position, shape,
colour, and opacity. These blobs are projected onto the screen and
blended in depth order, which maps naturally to GPU rasterisation and
achieves real-time frame rates at quality comparable to NeRF. The blobs
are initialised from a sparse point cloud produced by COLMAP, a standard
photogrammetry tool, and refined through optimisation.

In this application, 3DGS training runs on a desktop machine and the
resulting file is imported into a Metal-based viewer on the phone.
PocketGS~\cite{Guo26} shows that on-device training is beginning to
become feasible; the viewer is designed so that if on-device training
matures, it can be plugged in without changing the rendering side.

\section{Summary and Research Gap}
\label{sec:soa:gap}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Prior work fragments the capabilities needed for practical mobile interior
design. Consumer AR viewers lack a room model; research reconstruction
pipelines~\cite{dai2017scannet} produce semantics but not interactive,
on-device end-user workflows; standalone splat toolchains~\cite{kerbl20233dgs}
offer immersive viewing without parametric structure for furniture replacement.
Usability studies~\cite{kandil2021ux} further show that AR-only staging
workflows under-serve precision and persistence even when preview quality is
acceptable.

This thesis integrates the following checkpoints into one persistence-first
system on Apple mobile APIs, directly corresponding to the objectives in
Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metric typed room model} (Objective~1): semantic capture via
        RoomPlan~\cite{apple2024roomplan2}, yielding walls, openings, and
        object instances in a 'CapturedRoom' suitable for editing.
  \item \textbf{Persistent off-site editing} (Objective~2): a RealityKit
        scene graph saved to disk and edited without requiring the user to
        remain in the scanned space, so layout decisions can continue after
        capture on the same device or another session.
  \item \textbf{On-device asset authoring} (Objective~3): photogrammetric
        object reconstruction through Object Capture, with resulting USDZ or
        equivalent assets placed and scaled inside the staged room.
  \item \textbf{Gaussian splat viewing} (Objective~4): Metal-based splat
        rendering with an off-device training path and forward compatibility
        for on-device training.
  \item \textbf{DCC (Digital Content Creation) export} (Objective~5): export to Blender for downstream
        content pipelines.
  \item \textbf{Usability-informed interaction model}: a non-AR,
        persistence-first editor,
        with AR preview where appropriate rather than as the sole editing surface.
\end{itemize}

No surveyed consumer or research toolchain listed above delivers all six
together on a single mobile platform; closing that gap is the contribution
this thesis claims.
